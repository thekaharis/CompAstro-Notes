# Loss Objective and Operator Basis Sweep

Consolidated results across all three active tasks as of **2026-07-26**. Tables are computed by `util/make_findings_report.py` from `final_report.json` / `metrics.jsonl` / `run_metadata.json` in the checkpoint directories; the raw report lives at `wiki/thesis/notes/FINDINGS-2026-07-26.md` and supersedes the 07-21 version.

## Headline results

1. **Pure L² beats L²+H¹ on the $z_\text{re}$ task by ~35%** for *every* architecture tested — and improves the H¹ metric itself. The H¹ term was not buying gradient fidelity.
2. **Nominal loss weights were misleading all along.** At absolute-mode 0.5/0.5, the H¹ term supplies **99.4%** of the 3-D training loss (raw H¹ ≈ 180× raw L²); in relative mode ~88%. **Every archived "L²+H¹" 3-D run is effectively H¹-dominated** — a retroactive caveat on the whole 3-D leaderboard.
3. **SIREN-generated spectral weights transform Local-FNO.** On $z_\text{re}$ it moves from worst (RMSE 0.305) to second best (0.123), and the train→test gap collapses from ~3.0× to ~1.2×.
4. **Model capacity (width) is the strongest hyperparameter** on $z_\text{re}$; spectral bandwidth knobs do not help there — consistent with the mode-weight diagnostics in [[SirenFNO Spectral Bias Investigation]].
5. **The Walsh–Hadamard operator wins, but only in the GLOBAL slot.** On 2-D $x_\text{HI}$, `localop(fourier/hadamard)` is the best model so far (val RMSE 0.1453 vs Local-FNO 0.1487, beating U-FNO by ~9%). Putting the same operator in the *local* windowed slot consistently underperforms. See [[Walsh-Hadamard Neural Operator]].

### Local-SirenFNO sweep conclusions (14 runs)

- **Width saturates at 32.** bw16 → bw24 → bw32 improves monotonically; **bw48 is consistently *worse*** at matched settings, even on 300-epoch schedules.
- **Higher LR wins at higher width — but only over a full schedule.** At epoch ~50 the lr 1e-4 run led; the final ordering is 3e-4 > 2e-4 > 1e-4 at bw32. **Mid-run LR comparisons are unreliable** — a methodological lesson for the rest of the programme.
- **ω=60 helps at low width, not at high**: −6.5% at bw16, −2.4% at bw32/lr2e-4, neutral at bw48. ω=15 is clearly worse than the ω=30 default.
- **Bandwidth knobs hurt**: local modes 6→8 (`m88`) and window 16→32 with 12 modes (`win32m1212`) both land below baseline. **Do not transfer this to 3-D** — the $z_\text{re}$ target has 7–11× *less* small-scale transverse power than $x_\text{HI}$ slices.
- **Best config: bw32, ω=30, lr 3e-4, pure L², 300 epochs** → RMSE(z) 0.1233, beating U-FNO by 18% with ~54× fewer parameters. Plain FNO still leads at 0.1168 (a 5.6% gap).

### Failure modes found

- **SirenFNO NaN (2-D).** Mostly-zero target → sigmoid saturation (100% of outputs at the rails by batch ~40) → on fully-clamped cones prediction and target are both exactly 0 → the L² norm is exactly 0 → `PowBackward0` (the sqrt) returns NaN → weights poisoned. **Loss-agnostic** (pure L² fails too), and gradient clipping cannot help because NaN clips to NaN. Fixes: decouple the DC mode from the SIREN-generated weights, and use a **linear output head** (`SIREN_OUTPUT_SIGMOID=0`, now default).
- **Relative-mode L1+H2 froze.** Loss 2e11 at epoch 0, then constant 6.562121 from epoch 2: relative normalisation divides by a near-zero target norm, blows up the weights, saturates, gradients die. Use absolute mode for L1/H2 on this target.

All runs pure L² except `localwno_lr3e4_h1e3` (L²+0.001·H¹, val 0.1543). Full 13-run table in the raw note.

- **Best model: Walsh–Hadamard in the GLOBAL slot only.** `localop(fourier/hadamard)` at val 0.1453 / test 0.1502 is the **first run to beat plain Local-FNO** (0.1487) on this task. Both-slots Hadamard is a close second (0.1501); every *local*-slot-Hadamard variant sits **below** Local-FNO at 0.151–0.153.
- **The local/global split matters more than which basis.** Six WHNO configs varied slot (local / global / both), local mode count (6 vs 12), width (32 vs 48) and coefficient ordering (sequency vs natural). Ordering made little difference (0.1520 vs 0.1533 at matched settings); **moving the operator from local-only to global-only changed the result by ~5% — more than any other knob in the sweep.** Combined with LocalWNO (wavelet-local, Fourier-global) landing merely *level* with Local-FNO, the pattern across both structured-transform families is that **the global whole-field bottleneck is where a non-Fourier operator pays off**, not the windowed local branches.
- **U-FNO generalizes worst** (val/train 1.19× vs ~1.08× for the local models) — consistent with the $z_\text{re}$ and 3-D tasks.
- **Retraction of an earlier claim.** LocalWNO's apparent large lead over the 3-D baselines was mostly a **z-interpolation handicap on those baselines**, not the operator. Out-of-sample WNO still led (test-cone RMSE 0.214 vs U-FNO 0.241), but the same-task numbers above are the fair comparison. WNO's genuine edge is **stage-localized**: strongest where the field is mostly neutral with sparse compact bubbles (wavelet sparsity), weakest/negative when nearly fully ionized.

### Diagnostics

- **Mode-weight profiles (3-D Local-FNO).** Edge/peak weight ratio is 0.03–0.20 on the x and y axes but **0.60–0.78 on the LOS (z) axis** in the bottleneck and decoders. **The binding bandwidth constraint in 3-D is the LOS direction** — which the 2-D $z_\text{re}$ task structurally cannot inform. `LOCALFNO_MODES_Z` (12, cap 17) and the bottleneck z modes (16, cap 33) both have headroom.
- **Power spectra.** Both U-FNO and Local-FNO lose transverse power above $k_\perp \sim 1\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$ (the front-smoothing signature). Local-FNO additionally *gains* spurious low-k power at early epochs, growing with redshift — consistent with a window-grid artifact. Figures: `figures/ps_out/localfno-vs-ufno-contrastz7/`.
- **Boundary band.** Truth front width 3.6 Mpc; both models predict **12–14 Mpc**, i.e. fronts ~3.5× too smooth — *despite* the H¹-dominated loss. This is the observation the [[Contrast Map Sharpening]] investigation set out to explain, and it is now attributed to the objective rather than the bandwidth (see [[Hedging Bias of Pointwise Losses]]).

## 4. Infrastructure

- **$z_\text{re}$ input cache** (`dataset/zre_input_cache.py`, `slurm/build_zre_inputs.sbatch`): per-cone density slices + params in one HDF5. Startup **~2 h → minutes**; verified byte-identical to raw reads.
- **`metrics.jsonl` + `run_metadata.json` for $z_\text{re}$ runs** (`ZreLoggingTrainer`) — $z_\text{re}$ runs now appear on the dashboard.
- **Dashboard**: per-task pages (21cm 3-D / $z_\text{re}$), richer run labels, and a config comparison table that highlights only parameters that differ across selected runs.
- **Metadata backfill** (`util/backfill_zre_metadata.py`): reconstructs architecture fields for historical $z_\text{re}$ runs from job logs. ω was never logged, so for the historical sweep it is inferred from the directory name and flagged `siren_omega_inferred_from_dirname` — **treat ω in archived rows as inferred, not recorded.**
- **`fno_zre.py`** records the full architecture per model kind, supports `RESUME_DIR`, and applies `GRAD_CLIP_NORM` (default 1.0 for SIREN kinds).

## 5. Open questions

- Does **pure L² also win in 3-D**? Being measured (`checkpoints_3d_localsirenfno` vs `..._l2only`).
- Does the **$z_\text{re}$ width finding transfer**? bw32/bw48 with ω=60 running on the 3-D task.
- A genuinely **balanced L²+H¹ has never been tested in 3-D** — queued as `..._bw32om60_relbal` (relative mode, weights 0.5/0.07). Given headline 2, every historical "balanced" run was nothing of the kind.
- **LOS bandwidth** (`LOCALFNO_MODES_Z`) is the untested 3-D-specific lever indicated by the mode-weight diagnostic.
- Local-FNO (non-SIREN) **overfitting**: augmentation (random transverse rolls + D4 symmetries) and AdamW remain untried.
- Does **global-only Walsh–Hadamard transfer to 3-D and to $z_\text{re}$**? Untested — and the z-interpolation retraction above is a standing warning that a 2-D win is not a reliable predictor. Filed as [[Square-Wave Basis for Ionization Fields]].
